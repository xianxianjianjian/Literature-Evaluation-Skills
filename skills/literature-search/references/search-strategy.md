# Search Strategy

Search must be designed from the confirmed weekly topic and recorded well enough that another researcher can understand what was searched, where, when, and why.

## Search Question Profile

Before querying databases, record:

- confirmed topic;
- core research question;
- population/context when relevant;
- focal construct/phenomenon;
- relation, mechanism, comparison, or outcome of interest;
- preferred methods, paradigms, measurements, or indicators;
- preferred study type;
- date preference;
- known exclusions;
- transfer needs for the user's current research.

## Concept blocks

Construct separate English concept blocks rather than one over-constrained query. Typical blocks include:

1. focal phenomenon/construct;
2. relation, mechanism, outcome, or theoretical question;
3. method, paradigm, measurement, or indicator;
4. population/context when needed.

Include established synonyms, spelling variants, abbreviations, controlled vocabulary or subject headings when supported by the database. Test several combinations and record materially different query forms.

Do not silently narrow a search simply because one query returns too many results. Narrow only with a reason that matches the research question.

## Database routing

Choose sources by field and question. When available, aim for both:

- at least one field-specific or domain-relevant database/source;
- at least one broad, indexing, citation, or cross-disciplinary perspective.

Journal-specific searching and citation chaining may supplement database searching. Record databases or sources that were intended but unavailable; do not pretend they were searched.

## Literature roles and recency

Use role labels rather than one universal year cutoff:

- `CURRENT`: strongly prefer the most recent ~2 years when quality and fit are adequate; prime weekly candidates;
- `RECENT_SUPPORT`: recent supporting work, commonly ~3–5 years;
- `FOUNDATIONAL`: seminal theory, paradigm, measure, or method regardless of age;
- `BACKGROUND_REVIEW`: high-quality review/meta-level source used for field mapping rather than automatically chosen as the focal empirical paper.

Default recency behavior:

- frontier/current-development questions: emphasize roughly the latest 2–3 years;
- ordinary empirical questions: strong preference for the latest 2 years, widening to roughly 5 years when needed for quality, fit, or evidence coverage;
- foundational theory/method work: no age restriction.

Recency must not override major quality or relevance differences.

## Retrieval and legal access

Search may inspect bibliographic records, abstracts, publisher pages, repositories, and legally accessible full text. Do not bypass paywalls, authentication restrictions, or institutional access controls.

Do not bulk-download every candidate. Full Main/SI retrieval is prioritized for final-round candidates and especially the user-confirmed focal paper.

## Saturation and stop rule

Do not stop after finding the first attractive paper. Before stopping, check as appropriate:

- major synonyms and alternate query formulations;
- mapped Core/Recommended journals;
- recent competing studies;
- related/citing/cited work;
- recent reviews useful for field mapping;
- method-specific searches where method transfer is an explicit goal.

Search can stop when all planned source classes have been covered, additional synonym/journal/citation searches no longer yield materially new high-fit candidates, and the Primary/Strong Alternative ranking is stable enough for a user decision.

This is a weekly targeted search, not a systematic review. Do not label it PRISMA-compliant or exhaustive.

## `search_record.md` minimum record

For each meaningful search route record:

- date/time;
- database/source;
- query or search logic;
- filters/limits;
- result count when available;
- records retained for screening;
- source availability or access limitation;
- notes on new synonyms or direction changes.

The record should also contain the Search Question Profile, literature-role policy used, saturation rationale, and explicit unavailable-source notes.
