## Search Question Profile

- Core question: Across repeated nights, what reliability range is reported for PSG sleep architecture and representative sleep-EEG microstructure measures, and which indicators are sufficiently stable to inform a CountingSheep PSG test–retest reliability hypothesis?
- Population/context: prioritize healthy/community adult PSG or closely related repeated-night datasets; retain clinical populations when they provide unusually strong methodological evidence.
- Focal constructs: night-to-night variability, test–retest reliability, reproducibility, agreement, first-night effect.
- Macro metrics: TST, sleep efficiency, SOL, WASO, REM/N1/N2/N3/SWS percentages or duration, REM latency, arousal/micro-arousal indices.
- Micro metrics: spindle density/frequency/duration/amplitude, SWA/SOA, K-complex density/amplitude, spectral power; retain adjacent PSG-derived physiology when methodologically informative.
- Preferred reliability statistics: ICC with confidence intervals, SRD/MDC, Bland–Altman agreement, CV, variance decomposition; correlations alone are insufficient for the highest-priority focal role.
- Preferred study design: repeated-night PSG/EEG in the same individuals; direct standard PSG preferred for focal use.
- Recency: CURRENT strongly preferred (~2024–2026), widened to recent/supporting and foundational work when necessary.
- Transfer need: generate defensible expected reliability ranges and indicator choices for a CountingSheep PSG cross-night reliability study.

## Round 2 Detailed Screening and Ranking

Scores use 0–5 ratings normalized with the frozen weights: relevance 25, quality 20, journal/source 15, method transfer 15, current-research transfer 10, novelty 10, fulltext/SI 5.

### Primary — Mullins et al. 2025

- DOI: `10.1111/jsr.14281`
- Journal: *Journal of Sleep Research*, 34(1), e14281
- Literature role: `CURRENT`
- Quality Gate: `GREEN`
- Score components: relevance 5.0; quality 4.5; journal/source 4.5; method transfer 5.0; current-research transfer 5.0; novelty 4.5; fulltext/SI 5.0
- Weighted total: **95.5 / 100**
- Design: 107 cognitively normal older adults, two consecutive laboratory PSGs.
- Reliability methods: two-way single-scorer ICC with 95% CI, SRD, paired night comparisons, correlation visualization, Bland–Altman plots; Bonferroni-corrected alpha for multiple comparisons.
- Macro finding at screening level: TST, REM%, and sleep efficiency had low ICC; SWS% and NREM2% moderate.
- Micro finding at screening level: frontal/central SWA/SOA and K-complex measures generally good; spindle density central good and frontal/parietal excellent (>0.9 for frontal/parietal density).
- Source package: Version of Record verified at Wiley; PubMed/PMC record; full text legally available; Supporting Figure S1 available and contains Bland–Altman plots.
- Data/code/prereg: data available from corresponding author on reasonable request; no code/preregistration identified in current checks.
- Conflict statement: authors report no conflicts.
- Integrity check: current checks found no obvious retraction, expression of concern, DOI/version conflict, or correction issue. This is not a claim that the data are proven authentic.
- Method transfer: `DIRECTLY_REUSABLE` for ICC category interpretation, channel-specific microstructure reliability, reporting ICC + CI + agreement/SRD together; exact algorithms require Deep Reading before reuse.
- Main caveat: older-adult sample (55–80 range in SI figure description), so reliability magnitudes should not be assumed identical in younger samples.

### Strong Alternative 1 — Chouraki et al. 2023

- DOI: `10.1093/sleep/zsac319`
- Journal: *SLEEP*, 46(5), zsac319
- Literature role: `RECENT_SUPPORT`
- Quality Gate: `AMBER`
- Score components: relevance 4.0; quality 4.0; journal/source 4.5; method transfer 4.5; current-research transfer 4.5; novelty 4.5; fulltext/SI 5.0
- Weighted total: **86.0 / 100**
- Design: 94 healthy males, 5 consecutive weeknights, 470 nights, at-home Dreem headband EEG.
- Strength: directly quantifies metric-specific CV and derives minimum nights/sample-size guidance. Low variability for TST, sleep efficiency, N2/REM percentages and micro-arousal index; higher variability for SOL/WASO and some stage-duration/latency variables.
- Full text and supplementary figures available; deidentified data available on request under agreement.
- AMBER reason: not conventional full PSG across all nights; first night excluded from main variability reference; several authors are Dreem employees/consultants and one owns stock. These are disclosed conflicts, not an integrity failure.
- Method transfer: `DIRECTLY_REUSABLE` for thinking about metric-specific night requirements and design sensitivity; `REUSABLE_WITH_MODIFICATION` for direct CountingSheep ICC hypothesis because its principal variability metric is CV and device/context differ.

### Strong Alternative 2 — Yang et al. 2026

- DOI: `10.1007/s11325-026-03732-w`
- Journal: *Sleep and Breathing*, 30(3), 183
- Literature role: `CURRENT`
- Quality Gate: `AMBER`
- Score components: relevance 4.5; quality 4.0; journal/source 3.5; method transfer 4.5; current-research transfer 4.5; novelty 5.0; fulltext/SI 2.5
- Weighted total: **84.0 / 100**
- Design: 92 healthy participants, two consecutive PSG nights; macro FNE plus spindle characteristics and slow-oscillation/delta relative PSD.
- Strength: newest directly relevant healthy two-night macro+micro study; spindle characteristics were described as stable while macro FNE and SWA/SO changes were present.
- AMBER reason: the research question is first-night adaptation rather than formal test–retest reliability, and the current search established bibliographic/abstract access but not a complete legal full-text/SI package for focal Deep Reading.
- Integrity check: current PubMed record reports ethics approval and no competing financial interests; no obvious correction/retraction concern found in current checks.
- Method transfer: `REUSABLE_WITH_MODIFICATION`; useful for interpreting first-night effects and choosing spindle/SWA variables, but not a direct substitute for an ICC reliability study.

### Additional method-support candidates

- Levendowski et al. 2017, DOI `10.5664/jcsm.6618`: macro + spindle/frontopolar EEG stability with legal PMC full text; useful but AMBER because the repeated-night arm used a mixed clinical population and a vendor-developed device, and two authors were employees/shareholders of the device company.
- Reynolds et al. 2019, DOI `10.1111/jsr.12698`: nine-night spindle reliability in adolescents; very useful for demonstrating that spindle subfeatures require different numbers of nights, but n=12 and age/context limit direct focal use.
- Gaines et al. 2015, DOI `10.5665/sleep.5152`: foundational macro reliability evidence; single-night TST/SOL/WASO ICCs ~0.37–0.59 and 3-night averages ~0.64–0.81.
- Zheng et al. 2012, DOI `10.5664/jcsm.1670`: large repeated in-home PSG; continuity/duration measures showed lower ICC than delta sleep architecture measures; strong design reference with montage confounding across nights.

## Why Primary outranks the alternatives

Mullins 2025 is ranked first because it is the only high-fit recent candidate in this search that simultaneously provides: (1) two repeated standard laboratory PSGs; (2) both macrostructure and multiple EEG microstructure families; (3) a formal ICC reliability framework with confidence intervals; (4) an agreement metric (SRD) and Bland–Altman follow-up; (5) channel-specific microstructure results; and (6) a complete legally accessible Main + SI package. Chouraki 2023 is stronger for multi-night design/sample-size planning but uses a wearable context and focuses macro metrics/CV. Yang 2026 is newer and directly informative about first-night microstructure, but it is not primarily a formal test–retest reliability paper and the complete source package was not established in this run.
